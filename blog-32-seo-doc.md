## 11. Sections Covered

1. **Why Porcelain Labware Exists as a Separate Material Category** - Frames porcelain's material properties (high max temperature, low thermal shock resistance, opacity) as the basis for when it is the correct choice versus glass or plastic.

2. **Application 1: Crucibles for Gravimetric Ignition** - Why glass and plastic are physically ruled out at ignition-to-constant-weight temperatures.

3. **Application 2: Evaporating Dishes** - End-stage evaporation heat risk to glass; plastic ruled out entirely.

4. **Application 3: Spot Test Plates** - Visual contrast and chemical inertness advantages over plastic staining/degradation.

5. **Application 4: Combustion Boats** - Furnace-tube temperature requirements; note on alumina/advanced ceramics for extreme cases.

6. **Application 5: High-Temperature Filter Funnels** - Hot filtrate and fusion residue handling versus plastic degradation.

7. **Application 6: Mortars and Pestles** - Hardness/abrasion resistance for grinding versus plastic wear and glass chipping.

8. **Application 7: Muffle Furnace Trays** - Sustained high temperature tolerance; notes gradual furnace heating negates porcelain's thermal shock weakness.

9. **Application 8: Fusion Dishes** - Molten flux tolerance; when platinum/specialized ceramics are needed instead.

10. **Porcelain vs Glass vs Plastic: A Property Comparison** - 7-row table covering max temperature, thermal shock resistance, fusion/acid chemical resistance, hardness, transparency, and cost.

11. **How to Choose the Right Material for Your Application** - 3-question decision framework (temperature/flame/fusion, rapid thermal cycling, mechanical grinding); contains all 3 internal links.

12. **FAQ** - 7 questions covering crucible material choice, thermal shock handling, cost rationale versus plastic, chemical reactivity limits (HF, aggressive flux), main disadvantage (opacity/brittleness), classroom use guidance, and Medilab's own porcelain range.
