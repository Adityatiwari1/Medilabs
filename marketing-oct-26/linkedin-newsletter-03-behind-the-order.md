## Article body

You confirmed the purchase order. Then what?

For overseas buyers, the weeks after a PO are a black box. This edition opens it. Here is the sequence a MEDILAB export order follows from confirmation to dispatch, and what you can ask to see at each stage.

### 1. Order review and confirmation

The sales team checks the PO against the quotation: item codes, quantities, standards, tolerance classes, packing requirements and shipping terms. Anything unclear is raised in writing before work starts. A clear PO is the cheapest quality control there is.

### 2. Specification and artwork lock

For catalog items, the specification is confirmed against the data sheet. For custom or branded orders, artwork and marking are approved and recorded. Changes after this point cost time, so this is the moment to catch them.

### 3. Stock allocation or production planning

Items in stock are allocated to the order. Items that need manufacturing are scheduled. Where an order mixes both, the plan is built around the longest item so the shipment stays together.

### 4. Production and in-process checks

For manufactured items, the glass is formed, annealed and finished by skilled glassblowers and technicians. In-process inspection removes defects early, when rework is cheap.

### 5. Final quality review

Before packing, products are reviewed for visual defects, dimensions, markings and, for volumetric ware, volume against the stated class and standard. Items that need individual calibration certificates follow that route. Our quality system is certified to ISO 9001:2015.

### 6. Export packing

Each piece is protected by cells or sleeves, grouped in partitioned inner cartons, packed in strong outer cartons and secured on pallets where the load calls for it. Cartons carry product code, quantity, lot number and handling marks. Wooden pallets or crates are handled with international wood packaging rules in mind.

### 7. Documentation

The commercial invoice, packing list and certificates are prepared and cross-checked against the physical cartons. The HS code, quantities, lot numbers and product codes must match across every document, because mismatches are a common reason for customs queries.

### 8. Booking, dispatch and tracking

Freight is booked according to the agreed Incoterm. We share the shipping documents and tracking details so your team and your customs broker can prepare for arrival.

### What you can ask for at each stage

- A confirmation of the order review in writing
- Approved artwork or sample photographs
- Photographs of packed cartons and the loaded pallet
- Copies of the inspection record and certificates
- Draft shipping documents before dispatch

### Why we share this

Trust in international trade is built on visibility. You cannot walk the factory floor, so we try to bring it to you in documents and photographs. The more of this process you can see, the faster you can approve a supplier and the fewer surprises arrive with the container.
