## Article body (paste into the LinkedIn newsletter editor)

A flask says 100 mL on the glass. What proves it?

For most buyers the answer is a piece of paper. That is why the paper matters as much as the glass, and why one document in particular is worth understanding before you approve a supplier.

### What NABL accreditation actually covers

NABL is India's national accreditation body for testing and calibration laboratories. It assesses laboratories against ISO/IEC 17025, the international standard for technical competence.

Accreditation is granted for a defined scope. The scope lists what the laboratory may calibrate and how well. A certificate only carries the accreditation symbol when the work falls inside that scope.

This is different from an ISO 9001 certificate. ISO 9001 shows a company runs a documented quality system. ISO/IEC 17025 shows a laboratory can produce valid measurement results. Buyers need both, but only the second says anything about one specific flask.

### Two documents that look similar and are not

- **Batch certificate of conformance.** States that a production lot was sampled and meets a tolerance class. It describes the lot.
- **Individual calibration certificate.** Describes one serialized item. It records the measured volume, the uncertainty, the method and the traceability.

Most routine work is well served by batch-certified Class A glassware, because the tolerance already bounds the error. Individual certificates earn their cost when you prepare primary standards, when you apply a correction to the actual measured volume, or when a regulator or customer asks for item-level traceability.

### What to look for on the certificate

1. The laboratory name and accreditation number
2. A unique certificate identifier
3. The item identified by type, nominal volume and mark
4. The method and the 20 degrees C reference temperature
5. The measured volume with units
6. An expanded uncertainty statement, usually at k=2
7. A traceability statement
8. A date and an authorized signature

If two or more of these are missing, send the document back with a written query.

### Why it travels across borders

NABL is a signatory of the ILAC Mutual Recognition Arrangement. So are the accreditation bodies that US and Chinese laboratories rely on, including A2LA in the United States and CNAS in China. That shared framework gives your auditor a recognized basis for accepting the document without re-qualifying the calibration laboratory from scratch.

Acceptance is still your quality system's decision. The MRA gives you a defensible reason, which is the standard most auditors apply.

### A point for US buyers

ISO 1042 and ASTM E288 are not identical. Both define Class A volumetric flasks, but the published limits differ at several volumes, and ASTM E288 is tighter at the sizes most laboratories use. Name the standard on your purchase order. A certificate with a measured volume lets you demonstrate compliance with the stricter limit instead of relying on the class label alone.

### A point for buyers in China

CNAS also signs the ILAC MRA, so both systems rest on ISO/IEC 17025. State the standard and the acceptance criteria in the order, and ask the supplier to confirm which limits the measured volume was compared with.

### How to verify in five minutes

1. Find the accreditation number on the certificate.
2. Search the public NABL directory for the laboratory.
3. Confirm the accreditation is current on the calibration date.
4. Confirm the scope covers volumetric glassware.
5. Compare the uncertainty with the tolerance limit. The measured volume plus or minus the uncertainty should sit inside the limit.

Keep a copy of the check with the certificate. The evidence trail is what an auditor will ask to see.
