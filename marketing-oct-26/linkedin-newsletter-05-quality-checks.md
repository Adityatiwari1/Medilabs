## Article body

A broken or mislabeled shipment is rarely a surprise to the factory. It is usually a check that was skipped.

Here are the nine checks we think every glassware shipment should pass before dispatch. Use the list on us, and on any other supplier you are evaluating.

### 1. Material and lot verification

Is this the glass the order specified? Material statements and lot records link the finished product to a glass type and a production lot. Lot numbers on cartons must match the records so any later query can be traced.

### 2. Visual inspection

Trained inspectors check each piece under bright, uniform light for seeds, stones, cords, checks, chips and scratches. Rims, bases, necks and joints get extra attention because most mechanical failures start there. Written acceptance criteria keep decisions consistent between shifts.

### 3. Stress inspection

A polariscope reveals internal stress that the eye cannot see. Poor annealing is invisible until the first heating cycle, which is the worst time to find out.

### 4. Dimensions and joints

Height, diameter, neck size and wall thickness are measured. Standard taper joints are checked with gauges and stoppers are test-fitted. A joint that is slightly out of tolerance either leaks or seizes.

### 5. Marking and class

Volume, tolerance class, reference temperature, standard and lot code must be present, legible and permanent. A Class B item marked as Class A is a serious nonconformance, so class markings are checked against the order.

### 6. Volume verification

For volumetric ware the central test is gravimetric, as described in ISO 4787. Water at a recorded temperature is weighed on a calibrated balance and the volume is compared with the tolerance of the relevant standard. Items that need individual certificates follow our NABL-accredited calibration route.

### 7. Function

Burette stopcocks are tested for leakage and smooth turning. Pipettes are checked for delivery time. Ground joints are checked for fit. These are simple tests that dimensions alone cannot replace.

### 8. Cleanliness, counts and packing

Items are clean, counted against the packing list and packed in cells or sleeves, partitioned cartons and strong outer cartons. Cartons are checked for the right product, quantity, lot number and label.

### 9. Documents match the goods

The invoice, packing list, certificates and carton labels must describe the same goods. A reviewer who was not involved in packing does this check, because fresh eyes find errors the packing team no longer sees.

### What this means for you

You do not have to take our word for any of it. Ask for the records:

- Material and lot records
- Defect library and sampling record
- Stress check results
- Dimensional report
- Volume test records and balance calibration status
- Photographs of packed cartons
- The release file for your shipment

You can also appoint a third-party inspection agency to attend packing, and we will support them with access and documents.

### Your own side of the process

Inspect on arrival as well. Check cartons for external damage before opening, photograph any concern, compare contents with the packing list and test a sample from each lot. When both sides use the same criteria, disputes are rare and fast to resolve.
