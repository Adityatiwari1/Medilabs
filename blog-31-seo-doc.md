## 11. Sections Covered

1. **Why 2026 Is a Growth Window for Lab Equipment Distributors** - Frames the current buyer environment (consolidation, compliance pressure) as an opportunity/risk window for distributors who move deliberately vs. those who stand still.

2. **Strategy 1: Identify Catalogue Expansion Opportunities from Real Demand Signals** - Using declined customer requests and competitor comparisons as expansion evidence; prioritization framework (existing capability, demand volume, manufacturer availability).

3. **Strategy 2: Approach Manufacturer Partnerships as a Strategic Decision** - Evaluating manufacturer partners on certification, documentation, freight, and distributor-support willingness; questions to ask prospective partners.

4. **Strategy 3: Build a Genuine Multi-Brand Positioning Strategy** - Risk of single-manufacturer dependency; avoiding "undifferentiated reseller" trap via documentation-consistency positioning.

5. **Strategy 4: Understand Which Product Categories Travel Together** - Category pairing by institution type (hospital, university, pharma, industrial), using pairing data to sequence expansion.

6. **Strategy 5: Strengthen Documentation and Compliance as a Differentiator** - NABL/ISO 15189 pressure on buyers, standardizing certificate collection and pass-through across manufacturer partners.

7. **Strategy 6: Expand Institutional Account Relationships** - Growing existing accounts vs. new acquisition; multi-site/network expansion leverage.

8. **Strategy 7: Invest in Digital Catalogue and Quoting Infrastructure** - Quotation speed as a competitive factor; sequencing digital catalogue investment after quoting speed improvements.

9. **Category Pairing Reference by Buyer Type** - 5-row table mapping buyer type to core category, paired categories, and expansion priority.

10. **Applying This Guide to Your 2026 Growth Plan** - Sequencing guidance across the 7 strategies; contains internal links to blog-28, blog-30, and blog-27.

11. **FAQ** - 7 questions covering expansion prioritization, single vs multi-manufacturer strategy, pitching manufacturers as a smaller distributor, multi-brand reputation risk, digital catalogue importance vs pricing, documentation requirements, and Medilab's distributor partnership program.
