## 11. Sections Covered

1. **Why Multi-Vendor Lab Sourcing Costs More Than the Invoice Shows** - Frames the invisible-cost thesis: unit price comparison misses coordinator time, documentation, freight, and QC costs that accumulate across vendors.

2. **Cost 1: Coordinator and Administrative Time** - PO cycle multiplication per vendor, where coordinator hours actually go, exception-handling overhead across multiple account managers.

3. **Cost 2: Documentation Variation Across Vendors** - Certificate format inconsistency, audit trail compilation cost for NABL/ISO 15189 assessments, non-conformity risk from documentation gaps.

4. **Cost 3: Freight and Logistics Costs of Fragmented Ordering** - Per-shipment freight scaling, MOQ mismatches across vendors, customs clearance overhead for cross-border institutional buyers.

5. **Cost 4: QC Inconsistency Across Suppliers** - Variable QC baselines per vendor per category, receiving rejection rate impact, QMS complexity from multiple external quality standards.

6. **Cost 5: Total Cost of Ownership Comparison Table** - Side-by-side table across all four cost categories plus MOQ and vendor relationship, summarizing the structural savings case.

7. **What a True Single-Source Lab Supplier Must Cover** - Defines the 8-category range (glassware, plasticware, diagnostics, instruments, sterilization equipment, furniture, reagent storage, PPE) required for consolidation to actually work, with a caution against thin "full-range" positioning.

8. **How to Evaluate and Transition to Consolidated Sourcing** - Vendor mapping methodology, documentation sample evaluation, phased category-by-category transition approach. Contains all 3 internal links.

9. **FAQ** - 7 questions covering savings guarantees, vendor count thresholds, documentation requirements, multi-site hospital networks, evaluating category depth, Medilab's own range, and supply concentration risk.
